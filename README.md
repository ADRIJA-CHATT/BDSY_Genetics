Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified thousands of loci associated with complex traits, yet assigning these loci to effector genes remains difficult, as most associated variants are non-coding and correlated through linkage disequilibrium. Here we introduce a gene-prioritization framework that extends the Polygenic Priority Score (PoPS) by integrating sequence-based regulatory predictions of variant effects with nonlinear learning. For each gene within a set of defined loci, we aggregate AlphaGenome-predicted variant effects on RNA abundance across tissue and cell-type contexts. Variant contributions are weighted according to their distance from the gene body and optionally their CADD-predicted deleteriousness. With these gene-level aggregate-variant measures, we construct an orthogonal, GWAS-agnostic scoring metric for gene prioritization, using MAGMA gene-association statistics and random forest methods. When combined with other ranking systems, this model improved precision of both PoPS and MAGMA at the expense of recall. We later combine our AlphaGenome features with PoPS features, pathway and protein-interaction annotations, to extend our random-forest model, observing improved gene prioritization accuracy compared to PoPs across several traits. Together, these results elucidate the ability of predicted gene-level variant effects to enhance gene prioritization techniques by improving precision and accuracy in particular biological contexts. They open the door to further investigation in AlphaGenome-enhanced gene prioritization.
